# Privacy policy

Tasklace is a desktop application. Your plans stay on your computer, on the computers of the people you share them with and, encrypted, on the relay you choose if you share through one. Tasklace has no account, sends no usage statistics and shows no advertising.

This policy says what leaves your computer, when, and to whom.

## Checking for new versions

At most once a day, when Tasklace starts, it asks GitHub for the list of its releases, to tell you when a new version is out. It also asks when you choose “Check now” in Tasklace settings, even with the check turned off. It never updates itself: it only offers to open the page of the new release.

- **What GitHub receives**: the address of your Internet connection, as for any web request, and the version of Tasklace you use. Nothing about your plans or your computer is sent.
- **Who runs GitHub**: GitHub, Inc., under its own [privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- **How to turn it off**: open “Tasklace settings”, from the Tasklace menu at the end of the toolbar or at the foot of the welcome screen, and turn off “Check for new versions”. The installer, or the first launch when no installer asked, also lets you turn it off before anything is sent; until you chose, nothing is sent.

## Sharing a plan on your network

When you share a plan on your network, Tasklace announces it so that people near you can find it.

- **What computers on your network see**: the name of the plan and your name, as you typed them, the address and port of your computer, and an identifier of the shared plan that reveals nothing about its content. The join code and the key of the plan are never announced.
- **What the people who join receive**: the plan itself, encrypted, once they typed the right code.

Nothing is announced while you do not share.

## Sharing a plan through a relay

When you share a plan through a relay, the plan goes through the server whose address you chose, usually one run by your school or company.

- **What the relay sees**: the address of your Internet connection, when you connect, and the size of what you send. It also stores your changes, encrypted.
- **What the relay cannot see**: the plan, its name, the names of the people in it, or what they select. Everything is encrypted on your computer with keys that only the links you send hold.

How long the relay keeps encrypted plans, and what it records about connections, depends on the people who run it.

## What stays on your computer

- Your projects, their local copies and the list of recent projects.
- Your settings: your name for shared plans, the address of your relay, and your choices about files and new versions.
- Plans joined through a relay, and changes kept apart in a copy of your own.
- The links of plans shared through a relay, encrypted with the protected storage of your system, or not kept at all when your system has none.
- The log of errors, which helps to find the cause of a problem. Tasklace never sends it anywhere. If you add it to a bug report, read it first: it may name your files or plans.
